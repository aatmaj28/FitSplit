## 2026-10-15 hackathon build: photo tagging (#2) and image to layout (#4)

- [media] Claude budget for this build is "max 80 calls including testing". The ledger held 112 when it started, so CLAUDE_CALL_CAP now defaults to 192.
- [media, part 1] A media job's id is the hash of its uploaded files (plus the pipeline version). Uploading the same video again returns the stored job instantly. Every model answer is also cached by the hashes of the images sent (data/llm/cache/media), so re-running a step costs nothing.
- [media, part 1] Frames: about 2 per second with the ffmpeg binary bundled in imageio-ffmpeg (no system install needed; it reads iPhone .mov / HEVC). iPhone HDR clips (HLG / PQ) are tone mapped to SDR when the ffmpeg build has zscale, otherwise scaled as is. Frames are saved at 1024 px and sent to vision at 896 px.
- [media, part 1] Blur filter: Laplacian variance below 25, or for videos below 35% of the clip's median (motion blur is relative to the clip). Near duplicates: dHash within 6 bits of the last kept frame; the sharper one stays. At most 50 frames, thinned evenly, in walk order with timestamps. Photos keep upload order, get EXIF rotation applied, and HEIC opens via pillow-heif.
- [media, part 1] Tagging sends 10 frames per request: Claude and Gemini get separate images labelled "Photo N"; Groq (one image per request) gets a numbered contact sheet. A 50-frame video costs 5 calls. Values outside the fixed vocabularies (room types, 31 amenity ids, flooring, reference objects, #rrggbb colors) are dropped by validation, not trusted.
- [media, part 1] The amenity checklist merges frames: each amenity keeps its evidence frame ids, and confidence grows with the number of frames that show it (0.6 for one, +0.12 per extra frame, at most 0.95). Floor tags come from the flooring field, "ceiling fan" from objects, "natural light" needs at least two bright frames with windows.
- [media, part 1] "Common renter filter" is a curated flag in vocab.py (our judgement of which amenities listing sites usually offer as search filters). The insight counts how many found amenities carry it and are missing from the listing. It is a count, not a statistic or a traffic estimate.
- [media, part 1] Description: one cached text call (Claude first). An answer with a "$" or any number that isn't in the facts we sent is rejected, and a plain template is used instead.
- [media, part 1] One job runs at a time, in a background thread. Steps that aren't built yet show "skipped". The media upload limit is 500 MB (the floor plan parser keeps its 25 MB). Raw uploads (data/media_jobs/*/input) and data/media/ are gitignored; frames and job.json are kept so finished jobs work offline.
- [media, part 1] "Common renter filter" flags were narrowed to the amenities big listing sites plainly offer as search filters: in-unit laundry, washer/dryer hookups, dishwasher, central air, window AC, fireplace, hardwood floors, walk-in closet, furnished, balcony/patio. Stainless appliances, granite, microwave and the like are still tagged, just not counted as filters.
- [media, part 2] LeaseHub "New listing" has a "Floor plan" | "Video or photos" switch, kept in the URL (?source=video) so the guided demo can link straight to it. The floor plan path is unchanged.
- [media, part 2] Upload goes through XMLHttpRequest for real upload progress (fetch can't report it), then the page polls the job every second and shows the backend's own step list, details and progress. Steps not built yet are hidden rather than shown as skipped.
- [media, part 2] "Missing from your listing" compares what the photos show with what the listing already says: amenities the agent's pasted listing text mentions (simple keyword match in the browser), plus amenities already attached to that listing. For a brand-new listing everything found is new, which is true: untagged, the listing wouldn't show up under those filters. AC found twice (central and window) counts as one filter. Callout order puts laundry, dishwasher and air conditioning before floors (our judgement, not data).
- [media, part 2] Chips: hover shows the evidence photo, click opens every evidence photo, ✓ confirms, ✕ removes (with undo). Amenities seen in only one photo get a dashed outline and a "worth a quick check" note. No percentages are shown; "seen in N photos" is the honest form of the confidence.
- [media, part 2] A listing made from a video gets the id "media_<job>" and stores its amenities, description and cover photo in the shared store. Roombae and LeaseHub list it (cover photo instead of a plan drawing); it has no rent split or Apply until it has a floor plan. A video can also be attached to one of the six floor plan listings, which then shows "3D | Photos" tabs plus amenities and the description.
- [media, part 2] Media images try the backend first and fall back to the bundled copy (public/demo/media/<job>/) on error, so a listing published online still shows its photos offline.
- [media, part 3] Rooms: one vision call per job on numbered contact sheets (24 frames per sheet, 300 px tiles), with each frame's tags as text. The model returns rooms (frames, type, entrance) and connections, then code cleans it: every frame in exactly one room (strays join the nearest room in walk order), ids in walk order, names numbered when a type repeats ("Bedroom 1", "Bedroom 2"), and a connected graph. Two rooms the walker went straight between are connected even if the model missed it; rooms never walked between are chained in walk order and marked "assumed". Without a provider, runs of same-type frames are rooms and a one-frame blip joins its neighbours.
- [media, part 3] Panoramas: a room's longest run of frames (at most 8, at 800 px) goes through OpenCV's Stitcher in PANORAMA mode; if that fails, the last 8 and then the first 4. Black borders are cropped. A result barely wider than one frame, or stretched past 7:1, is rejected. Rooms that don't stitch (walking adds parallax) show their photos instead, with swipe and arrows.
- [media, part 3] Door hotspots sit where the walker crossed between two rooms (the pair of frames closest together in walk order), located in the panorama with ORB features and a homography. Doors closer than 12% of the panorama width are pushed apart.
- [media, part 3] The viewer is a flat panorama you drag along, not a 360° sphere: phone pans rarely cover 360°, and a flat strip shows what was filmed honestly. It has arrows at the doors, a fade between rooms, Back, ←/→ to look, ↑ through the door in view, ↓ back, edge chevrons, and a minimap. Before Part 4 the minimap lists the rooms in walk order; with an estimated layout it draws the rooms.
- [media, part 3] Listing pages get a "Walkthrough" tab next to "3D" (default for listings made from a video), and the LeaseHub results screen has "Amenities | Walkthrough" tabs.
- [media, part 4] Sizes: one vision call per room (at most 10 rooms) with up to 4 frames picked across its part of the walk, preferring sharp frames with reference objects (doors, fridge, stove, counter, toilet, tub, beds). The prompt gives standard sizes (door 80 x 30-32 in, fridge 30-36 in, range 30 in, counter 36 in high, toilet 28-30 in deep, tub 60 x 30 in, bed sizes, 8-9 ft ceilings) and asks for width, length, confidence and ±%.
- [media, part 4] Each answer is checked in code: the short side comes first; sizes outside a sane range for the room type are pulled into it and marked low confidence ("Adjusted from … to a sane range"). ± follows the confidence: high 8-15%, medium 15-25%, low 25-45%. With no valid answer the room gets a typical size, marked "typical" at ±40%, never passed off as measured.
- [media, part 4] Layout: rooms are placed breadth first from the entrance, each against a room it connects to (at least 3 ft of shared wall for a door), with no overlaps. Candidates favour a compact footprint, touching the room's other neighbours and lining up with existing walls. If nothing fits, a 3.5 ft hallway bridges the gap, or a corridor runs straight out; a connector that just extends a real hallway makes that hallway longer, at ±30% or more, with a note.
- [media, part 4] Doors go on shared walls: 33 in for doors, wider for open plan ("opening"). The entry goes on the entrance room's longest outside wall, and windows go on outside walls of rooms whose photos show windows. A bedroom whose only bathroom connects to nothing else gets "private bath" (for the rent split).
- [media, part 4] Output is the parsed-plan format (walls traced from a raster of room outlines with the openings cut out, rooms as rectangles, openings, footprint, scale 20 px/ft), so the 3D viewer, furniture fit and rent split run on it unchanged. "layout_source": "estimated_from_photos", per-room "dims_confidence" (level, ±%, reasoning, references, frames), area_source "estimated", printed_dims_text "≈ 11' × 12'". plan.png is the viewer's 2D overlay, and plan.svg is drawn from the same geometry.
- [media, part 4] Honesty in the UI: every size from photos shows "≈" and the ± range. There is an "Estimated from photos ±N%" badge on the 3D view, in the rent split, on the listing header and in the room table. The room panel shows confidence, the model's reasoning and the objects it measured against. Total sq ft is not filled into the listing form; it shows as a hint ("≈ 666 from photos"), so a published number is the agent's own. The AI rent check is off for estimated plans.
- [media, part 4] Estimated sizes are interior sizes. Each room's rectangle sits on the wall centerlines, half a wall thicker on every side, and the plan's room polygon is the floor between the walls. Before this, walls ate 3 in into every room, so the fit check rejected any furniture placed against a wall.
- [media, part 5] "Lived-in" is a toolbar toggle and is on by default on listing pages (Roombae, LeaseHub, the results screen). The parser lab and the flows that embed the viewer elsewhere keep the plan colors, so nothing there changes. Floors become canvas-drawn materials: hardwood, laminate, vinyl, tile, stone, carpet, concrete, deck. No image assets, so it works offline. Each wall face is painted with the room it faces, using vertex colors on the existing wall meshes, so the cutaway and 2D overlay keep working.
- [media, part 5] Paint and floor come from the photos when the listing has them: the median tagged wall color of the room's frames, kept light (lightness at least 0.78, saturation at most 0.35, because photos under indoor light read darker), and the most common tagged flooring. Otherwise a soft per-room palette is used, with hardwood in rooms and tile in kitchens and baths. The legend says which ("Walls and floors from the photos" / "Walls: soft palette").
- [media, part 5] A video attached to a floor plan listing is matched to plan rooms by type: the plan's bedrooms, largest first, take the video's bedrooms, most photographed first. The same goes for kitchens, baths and the rest. A plan estimated from photos already knows each room's frames. Clicking a room shows its photos in the room panel.
- [media, part 5] "Furnished" places a set per room type with the existing auto-place and fit check: bed sized to the room (queen from 130 sq ft, full from 95), nightstand, dresser and a desk in larger bedrooms; sofa, coffee table and TV stand in living rooms; a table in dining rooms and eat-in kitchens. Pieces that could only go in front of a doorway are dropped. It's a view, not saved, and edit mode still shows your own layout.
- [media, part 4, real video] The first real video showed a placement flaw. Its kitchen was boxed in on all four sides before its own neighbours (a bedroom and the bathroom) were placed, so they floated off to the side. Placement now looks one step ahead: a position is penalised for every unplaced neighbour, of this room or of any nearby room already placed, that would have no free wall left. All 9 rooms then touch the rooms they connect to.
- [media, part 4] A wall counts as outside (for windows and the entry) only if no other room is within 2 ft in front of it, so no window looks into the sliver between two rooms.
- [media, part 2, real video] The description model may only say what the facts say. A code check rejects "private", "spacious", "quiet", "luxury", "views of", "steps from", "walk to", "minutes", "close to" and "near" unless the facts contain them (the first real description said "private balcony"). The facts now say which room each amenity was photographed in, so a ceiling fan isn't moved to the bathroom, and the prompt asks to leave out temporary mess (paint debris). Claude Sonnet counts its thinking against max_tokens, so 900 truncated a 110-word answer and the chain fell back to Groq; the limit is now 3000.
- [media] Job folders (data/media_jobs/) are runtime data and are no longer kept in git. Instead, the demo video's results are bundled in frontend/public/demo/media/ for offline use, and its model answers are committed in data/llm/cache/media/, so re-running the same video costs no calls.
